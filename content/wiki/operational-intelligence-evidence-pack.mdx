## Evidence Standard

The evidence standard is intentionally adversarial: a claim is stronger only when it can be challenged by a benchmark, a counterexample, a practitioner review, a replay, or a measurable failure mode.

Useful evidence classes:

- Implementation example: a public-safe walkthrough showing how signals become transaction context, evidence, hypotheses, decision packets, and operator-approved action.
- Benchmark fixture: a repeatable scenario with expected behavior, refusal boundaries, citations, and acceptance criteria.
- Control comparison: a baseline against dashboard-only investigation, chatbot-only explanation, or unstructured incident notes.
- Practitioner review: structured feedback from SRE, architecture, executive, governance, and AI engineering perspectives.
- Regression history: tracked behavior changes over time, including failures and corrected gaps.
- Negative case: a scenario where Operational Intelligence should refuse, redirect, or declare insufficient evidence.

## OI-ROOM-001 Benchmark Rubric

OI-ROOM-001 should be judged on evidence handling, not visual impressiveness.

- Retrieval: does the system retrieve the doctrine, reference architecture, and relevant OI-ROOM-001 artifact?
- Grounding: does every material claim trace to public-safe context?
- Citation: are source links present, relevant, and reachable?
- Evidence attribution: are observations, inferences, contradictions, missing evidence, and confirmed facts visibly separated?
- Hypothesis lifecycle: do hypotheses move through proposed, supported, contradicted, rejected, or confirmed states?
- Contradiction handling: does contradictory evidence weaken or redirect the hypothesis?
- Missing evidence: does the system explicitly state what is unknown?
- Decision packet safety: are actions framed as review packets instead of hidden automation?
- Operator approval: are consequential actions gated by explicit human approval?
- Replay reproducibility: can the same scenario be re-run from an approved replay seed?
- Refusal: does the system refuse confidential or unsupported questions without exposing sensitive context?

## Practitioner Review Model

The strongest next evidence is not another page. It is structured review from people who would actually operate, govern, or buy the system.

- SRE reviewer: can this reduce investigation ambiguity without hiding uncertainty?
- Principal architect: are layer boundaries precise enough for independent implementation?
- AI engineer: are evaluation gates specific enough to catch regressions?
- Governance reviewer: are approval, refusal, retention, and provenance boundaries inspectable?
- Executive reviewer: does the model explain operational risk in decision language?

## Control Cases

Operational Intelligence should be compared against weaker and adjacent modes:

- Dashboard-only: fast visibility, weak reasoning trace, high operator synthesis burden.
- Chatbot-only: fluent summary, weak provenance, unclear approval boundaries.
- Incident-ticket-only: durable record, delayed learning, inconsistent hypothesis handling.
- Operational Intelligence workflow: slower than a raw answer, but stronger on evidence, uncertainty, reviewability, and learning.

## Evidence Ledger

Every public claim about the doctrine should eventually map to an evidence ledger entry.

- Claim
- Classification: established, derived, original, speculative, unsupported
- Evidence source
- Test or review method
- Result
- Limitations
- Next improvement

This turns seri.ai from a polished explanation into a body of work that can improve under pressure.
