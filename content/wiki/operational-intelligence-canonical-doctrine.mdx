Operational Intelligence is the reasoning layer between enterprise telemetry and human decision. It converts signals, transaction context, topology, evidence, historical memory, evaluation gates, and operator judgment into reviewable operational understanding.

Operational Intelligence is not a replacement for observability, incident management, SRE practice, ITSM, or human command. Observability makes systems visible. Incident management coordinates response. SRE improves reliability through engineering discipline. ITSM provides service management process. Operational Intelligence connects those foundations into an inspectable reasoning system for understanding what changed, what is affected, what evidence supports a hypothesis, what remains unknown, what action requires review, and what should be learned.

Core problem. Enterprises already have dashboards, logs, traces, tickets, chats, runbooks, alerts, postmortems, service maps, and change records. The hard problem is not collecting more operational fragments. The hard problem is turning those fragments into shared judgment under uncertainty. Operational Intelligence addresses the gap between raw visibility and accountable decision-making.

Adjacent domains. Observability provides telemetry such as traces, metrics, and logs. AIOps applies analytics and automation to IT operations. AgentOps focuses on observing and governing AI agents. Incident management organizes response and restoration. Knowledge graphs model relationships. AI evaluation measures system behavior. Operational Intelligence overlaps with each of these, but its specific concern is the decision path: how evidence becomes operational meaning, how hypotheses evolve, how action remains bounded, and how learning compounds.

Foundational citations versus original contribution. The doctrine builds on established work in SRE, observability, incident response, provenance, knowledge graphs, and AI risk management. Those foundations should be cited explicitly. The original contribution is the synthesis: a ten-layer operating model for enterprise operational reasoning, centered on transaction-aware evidence, hypothesis lifecycle, replay seeds, evaluation gates, and the operator control plane.

Claim classification ledger. The doctrine should make its evidence posture visible. Established claims should cite existing practice. Derived claims should show which established practices are being combined. Original claims should be framed as a synthesis to test, not as settled industry fact. Speculative claims should name what evidence would strengthen or falsify them. Unsupported claims should be removed or converted into testable questions.

Ten-layer framework. Signal Layer captures telemetry, alerts, logs, traces, events, changes, tickets, documents, and business process signals. Input: emitted operational data. Output: normalized candidate signals. Operator question: what changed or degraded? Failure mode: noisy signal treated as meaning.

Transaction Layer reconstructs customer, process, or business journeys across services and time. Input: traces, identifiers, workflow state, timestamps, and domain events. Output: transaction path, affected step, timing, and state change. Operator question: what journey is affected? Failure mode: local component view mistaken for business impact.

Topology Layer maps services, dependencies, owners, paths, policies, and blast radius. Input: service maps, dependency records, ownership, deployment context, and runtime paths. Output: constrained investigation scope. Operator question: where could this symptom propagate? Failure mode: stale topology creates false confidence.

Evidence Layer separates observation, inference, contradiction, missing evidence, and confirmed fact. Input: signals, traces, change records, runbooks, ticket history, and validated human notes. Output: cited evidence graph. Operator question: what supports or challenges the claim? Failure mode: conclusions presented without receipts.

Reasoning Layer proposes, compares, updates, rejects, and confirms hypotheses. Input: evidence graph, topology, transaction path, memory, and constraints. Output: hypothesis state with confidence movement and falsification criteria. Operator question: what explanation best fits the evidence, and what would disprove it? Failure mode: narrative fluency confused with investigation quality.

Memory Layer preserves reusable operational context, known failure modes, prior decisions, replay seeds, and post-incident learning. Input: approved incident records, decisions, patterns, and outcomes. Output: public-safe or permission-aware operational memory. Operator question: have we seen this pattern before? Failure mode: stale memory or private context contaminates reasoning.

Evaluation Layer tests the assistant, workflow, retrieval, refusal behavior, citation quality, and recommendation boundaries. Input: fixtures, replay seeds, expected behavior, known limitations, and regression history. Output: release gates and trust signals. Operator question: is this behavior reliable enough for the next level of responsibility? Failure mode: aggregate scores imply trust without proving behavior.

Decision Layer turns evidence-backed reasoning into reviewable options. Input: hypotheses, impact, risk, reversibility, ownership, policy, and evaluation status. Output: recommended review packet, mitigation option, escalation, or refusal. Operator question: what action is justified now? Failure mode: premature automation or recommendation without risk context.

Learning Layer updates patterns, memory, evaluation fixtures, documents, and future retrieval after outcomes are known. Input: operator feedback, post-incident review, accepted/rejected recommendations, and measured outcomes. Output: improved doctrine, patterns, and evaluation coverage. Operator question: what should become easier next time? Failure mode: every incident starts from zero.

Operator Layer keeps humans in control of consequential judgment. Input: evidence, recommendation, policy boundary, approval requirement, uncertainty, and blast radius. Output: approved action, escalation, rejection, or request for more evidence. Operator question: what must a responsible human decide? Failure mode: hidden autonomy, unclear accountability, or unsafe delegation.

Layer interactions. Signal without Transaction creates noise. Transaction without Topology lacks blast-radius discipline. Topology without Evidence becomes guesswork. Evidence without Reasoning becomes a pile of facts. Reasoning without Memory repeats the same investigations. Memory without Evaluation preserves bad habits. Evaluation without Decision has no operational consequence. Decision without Operator control becomes unsafe automation. Learning closes the loop so the next investigation starts with more context.

Synthetic case OI-ROOM-001. A checkout journey shows elevated latency and partial failures during a narrow window. Signal Layer detects latency, error-rate movement, and a related deployment event. Transaction Layer reconstructs the checkout path and shows the delay appears after a payment authorization hop. Topology Layer narrows the affected path to a dependency boundary and identifies ownership. Evidence Layer marks observed facts, a contradictory metric, and a missing trace segment. Reasoning Layer proposes multiple hypotheses: recent configuration change, downstream dependency degradation, or instrumentation artifact. Memory Layer retrieves prior known failure patterns without treating them as proof. Evaluation Layer checks whether the assistant cites sources, names uncertainty, handles contradiction, and refuses private or unsupported claims. Decision Layer drafts a rollback review packet and a dependency escalation option. Operator Layer requires explicit human approval before any consequential action. Learning Layer converts the final reviewed outcome into a replay seed, evaluation fixture, and future retrieval note.

Observation, inference, confirmed fact. An observation is directly present in approved evidence. An inference explains what the evidence may mean. A confirmed fact is validated by enough cited evidence or responsible human review. Operational Intelligence should label these states visibly and avoid collapsing them into one answer.

Glossary. Operational Intelligence: the reasoning layer between enterprise telemetry and human decision. Transaction Intelligence: reasoning over customer, process, or business journeys rather than isolated components. Evidence Graph: a typed graph of observations, sources, relationships, contradictions, missing evidence, and confirmed facts. Hypothesis Lifecycle: the movement of possible explanations through proposed, supported, contradicted, rejected, or confirmed states. Replay Seed: the minimum approved context needed to reproduce an investigation scenario without exposing production systems. Evaluation Gate: a release or responsibility boundary based on tested behavior, not confidence prose. Operator Control Plane: the human review layer that governs scope, approval, escalation, and accountability. Operational Memory: approved reusable context accumulated from prior incidents, decisions, patterns, outcomes, and lessons.

Boundaries. Operational Intelligence does not prove causality from sequence alone. It does not eliminate dashboards. It does not replace incident commanders, SRE judgment, ITSM processes, security incident response, or organizational accountability. It does not justify autonomous production changes without approval. It does not ingest confidential employer material, private logs, internal dashboards, proprietary project names, or unapproved architecture. It should refuse or redirect when the answer requires confidential context or unsupported claims.
